## 1. Bottom line

**Do not sign either document in its current form.** Neither is unusual for a private credit lender's
opening draft, but as drafted the package is **materially one‑sided** and contains at least
**five provisions that would cause us real harm**, plus **two that the lender's own attorney has
already admitted are non‑compliant** with the National Credit Act.

The three things that matter most:

1. **The Sale of Shares proceeds are ceded to Plane Tree "out and out" — outright, not as security.**
   That is a transfer of ownership of the claim from signature date. There is **no clause returning
   the claim to Tyron when the loan is repaid**, and **no clause obliging Plane Tree to pay back any
   surplus** above what we owe. If the De Jongh / Nel share sale is worth more than R2.4m, the excess
   is, on the face of the drafting, gone. *(Pledge cl 4.2, and Annexure A.)*

2. **There is no "ordinary course of business" carve‑out anywhere in the security document.** All
   present and future book debts, *and the credit balance in Tyron's bank account*, are ceded; and
   cl 6.1 then forbids him from "redirecting, withdrawing, transferring, setting off or otherwise
   dealing with" any of it without Plane Tree's prior written consent. Read literally, **Tyron cannot
   collect his own debtors or spend his own money from day one**, and is in technical default the
   moment he does. This is almost certainly not what they intend — but it is what the words say.

3. **This is a personal loan with unlimited recourse.** There is no company borrower, no limited
   recourse, no cap. Tyron is personally liable for R2.4m plus interest, secured on his vehicle, his
   plant, his receivables, his bank account and his insurance proceeds, and he warrants things
   (below) that no person can truthfully warrant for twelve months.

The email asking for **"company details to be used in the AOD/invoice for legal fees"** should not be
answered with company details. See §3 — it is the single most urgent item, and their own attorney has
written in the margin that it "is not strictly permitted".

## 2. What this actually costs

The Cost of Credit table leaves **TOTAL INTEREST, FEES AND CHARGES** and **TOTAL COST OF CREDIT** as
`R[●]` — blank. Their comment 10 asks *us* to supply the figure. Here it is, modelled on the
agreement's own terms (R2.4m, 21% nominal compounded daily on a 365‑day year, payments applied to
interest first per cl 8.3):

| | Drawdown 20 Aug 2026 | Drawdown 15 Sept 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 12 × R100 000 instalments | R1 200 000 | R1 100 000 *(first instalment missed — see below)* |
| **Balloon due 31 Aug 2027** | **≈ R1 632 126** | **≈ R1 711 262** |
| Total interest | ≈ R432 126 | ≈ R411 262 |
| **Total repaid** | **≈ R2 832 126** | **≈ R2 811 262** |

**Three things follow from this:**

- **The instalment schedule is a disguised balloon.** R100 000 a month barely exceeds the interest
  accrual (~R42–47k/month), so capital hardly moves. Roughly **68% of the principal falls due in a
  single payment on 31 August 2027.** The repayment structure in cl 8.1 reads like an amortising
  loan; it is not. Our refinancing/exit plan has to be built around a ~R1.63m bullet, not around
  twelve instalments.

- **"21% compounded daily" is not 21%.** Compounded daily, the effective annual rate is
  **23.36%**, not 21%. Their own attorney twice notes (comments 8 and 19) that "the maximum interest
  rate which can be charged is the Repo Rate plus 14%. That is 21% at the moment and we cannot charge
  24%." **They have written a 21% headline that compounds to 23.36% — which is the very outcome the
  comment says they cannot have.** Demand either simple interest or, at most, monthly capitalisation,
  and demand the effective annual rate be stated on the face of the agreement.

- **The dates are hard‑coded and will break.** cl 8.1.1 fixes the first R100 000 instalment at
  **31 August 2026**. But cl 5.1 gives 14 calendar days from signature for suspensive conditions
  (extendable by Plane Tree "in its sole and absolute discretion"), and cl 6.2 then gives them a
  further 14 days to pay out. On any realistic timeline **the first instalment falls due before the
  money arrives** — and cl 14.1.2 makes non‑payment on due date an Event of Default, which under
  cl 8.2 accelerates the whole R2.4m. **Every payment date must be keyed to the actual drawdown date
  ("the last day of the first calendar month following the Drawdown Date"), never to a fixed
  calendar date.** This is the cheapest and most important single amendment in the package.

## 3. URGENT — the "AOD / invoice for legal fees" request

Toivo's email asks us to send **"your company details to be used in the AOD/invoice for legal fees."**
Do not send them. Three reasons, in ascending order of seriousness:

**(a) We are probably not liable for these fees at all.** Loan cl 16 and Pledge cl 9 say the Borrower
pays all costs of drafting and implementing the agreements. Their own attorney's **comment 49** says
in terms:

> *"In terms of section 100 of the National Credit Act a credit provider must not charge an amount to
> the consumer in respect of any fee, charge, commission, expense or other amount payable by the
> credit provider to any third party… We will need to address this as we cannot charge the consumer
> the costs for the preparation of this agreement."*

**(b) The company‑invoice route is that same attorney's stated workaround — and he flagged it as
improper.** Comment 49 continues:

> *"We can possibly have his company pay these amounts but it is not strictly permitted."*

The request in Toivo's email is the execution of that workaround. If we supply company details we
become a knowing participant in routing a charge around a statutory prohibition. That is bad for us
twice over: we pay money we may not owe, **and** we create a paper trail showing both parties knew.

**(c) An AOD is a separate, independently enforceable debt.** An Acknowledgement of Debt is typically
a liquid document — it can support provisional sentence or summary judgment, often with a consent to
judgment attached, and it sits **outside** the NCA protections (s129 notice, debt review, in duplum)
that the loan agreement itself gives us. Signing one for legal fees hands Plane Tree a fast‑track
judgment route for a debt that has nothing to do with the facility.

**Note also:** the Cost of Credit table records **INITIATION FEE: NIL** and **SERVICE FEE: NIL**.
They have contractually agreed to charge us nothing, and are now seeking the same money through a
side document. Hold them to the table.

**Recommended response:** do not decline rudely and do not send company details. Ask three questions
(draft wording in §8): what is the quantum, on what basis is the Borrower liable given s100, and why
is an AOD required rather than the fee simply being disclosed in the Cost of Credit table. In most
cases that ends the request.

## 4. Loan Facility Agreement — provisions that harm us

Ranked by exposure. **🔴 = must change before signature. 🟠 = strongly press. 🟡 = raise and trade.**

### 🔴 4.1 Fixed calendar payment dates vs. floating drawdown *(cl 5.1, 6.2, 8.1, 14.1.2)*
Covered in §2. First instalment can fall due before funding. **Fix:** all dates keyed to Drawdown
Date; add a longstop date by which Plane Tree must fund, failing which we may walk away without cost.

### 🔴 4.2 "Any breach" is an Event of Default, with no materiality and no cure period *(cl 14.1.3)*
`14.1.3` makes **any** breach of **any** term an Event of Default. There is **no remedy period
anywhere in the agreement**. Combined with:
- `11.1.4` — any failure to provide "accurate, complete or timely" information is a *material* breach;
- `14.1.9` — "does anything which **may** prejudice the Lender's rights… **or omits to take steps to
  prevent** such prejudice" — an open‑ended, subjective trigger;
- `14.1.7` — "or becomes **financially distressed**", a Companies Act s128 concept that has no defined
  meaning for a natural person;

…Plane Tree can declare default on a wide range of ordinary events and accelerate the full R2.4m.
**Fix:** insert a materiality qualifier and a **10 business day written remedy period** for all
non‑payment breaches (5 business days for payment); delete "financially distressed"; delete or
narrow 14.1.9.

### 🔴 4.3 Warranties we cannot truthfully give for 12 months *(cl 12.1)*
Each of these is an absolute, continuing warranty, and a breach is an Event of Default under 14.1.8:

| Clause | Warranty | Why it is dangerous |
|---|---|---|
| 12.1.6 | Full tax compliance — all returns filed, all amounts paid to SARS | Any assessment, objection, or late return, however small, = untrue representation = acceleration. **Fix:** "in all material respects, save as disclosed in writing". |
| 12.1.3 | "not over indebted" | This is drafted to **destroy our reckless‑credit defence** under NCA s80–83 before it can be used. Their comment 31 admits the affordability assessment has not yet been done. **Fix:** delete, or reduce to a statement of belief as at signature date only. |
| 12.1.7 | No security over **any** asset in favour of **any** other creditor, for the whole term | Blanket negative pledge with no basket and no carve‑outs. Freezes all other finance — including asset finance on new equipment — for 12+ months. **Fix:** carve out (i) existing security, (ii) purchase‑money/asset finance on newly acquired equipment, (iii) ordinary trade credit, (iv) a de‑minimis basket. |
| 12.1.4 | "an experienced businessman… agrees the terms are in **plan** language" | Drafted to defeat a plain‑language challenge under NCA s64. Also contains a typo ("plan"). Low cost to leave, but note what it is doing. |
| 12.1.13 | Capable of performing obligations under the agreement **and every collateral agreement** | Extends the warranty into agreements we may not fully control. **Fix:** limit to this agreement. |

### 🔴 4.4 Electronic instructions — we carry 100% of the fraud risk *(cl 15)*
Plane Tree "will act on the instruction **without verifying** that the instruction was actually sent
by or under the direction of an authorised representative", and we **indemnify them** for any loss
arising from them acting on an instruction that merely *"appears to have been issued"* by us.

In the current business‑email‑compromise environment this is a live, uncapped exposure: a spoofed
email redirecting a payment or changing account details lands on us in full, even where a simple
callback would have caught it. **Fix:** require telephonic callback verification to a pre‑agreed
number for any payment instruction or change of banking details, and carve the Lender's own
negligence out of the indemnity.

### 🟠 4.5 Disputes about what we owe are decided by *their* accountant *(cl 18)*
Any dispute about the Aggregate Debt, interest, or the Interest Rate goes to **"the Lender's
accountant appointed to the review or audit of its financial statements"**, acting as expert, whose
determination is **"final and binding"** and reviewable only for "manifest typographical and/or
arithmetical error". Layered on top of:
- `14.4` — a **certificate signed by any director of Plane Tree** is *prima facie* proof of what we owe;
- `8.6.2` / `8.1` — payment "without deduction, set‑off, demand or delay by counterclaim".

Together these leave us with almost no practical route to challenge a balance. **Fix:** independent
expert nominated by SAICA (or the chairman of the relevant professional body) if the parties cannot
agree; costs shared; both parties entitled to make written representations; carve out manifest error
generally, not just arithmetic. And the certificate should be signed by an **independent auditor**,
not a director, and be *prima facie* proof only for interim relief.

### 🟠 4.6 Suspensive conditions run entirely their way *(cl 5)*
- 5.1 — conditions must be fulfilled "**to the reasonable satisfaction of the Lender**" within 14 days,
  "or such longer period as the Lender may, **in its sole and absolute discretion**, decide". We stay
  bound indefinitely while they decide.
- 5.4 — they may waive any condition "**with or without notice**" to us. We may not know what
  agreement we are in.
- 5.3 — our failure to comply is **"an Event of Default alternatively this Agreement shall not come
  into force"**. These are mutually exclusive and they pick. **You cannot default on an agreement
  that never came into force.** As drafted they could take the position that a condition we failed
  triggers acceleration of a loan that was never advanced.

**Fix:** hard longstop date; either party may walk away if unfulfilled; waiver requires written
notice; **delete "Event of Default" from 5.3** — failure of a suspensive condition should cause the
agreement to lapse, nothing more.

### 🟠 4.7 Attorney‑and‑own‑client costs "irrespective of whether legal action results" *(cl 14.3)*
Costs on the highest scale, including pre‑litigation costs, whether or not anything is ever litigated,
with consent to taxation. This sits uncomfortably with the NCA's limits on collection costs
(s101(1)(g), and Chapter 6 Part C, which the Cost of Credit table itself cites). **Fix:** reduce to
attorney‑and‑client scale, delete "irrespective of whether or not legal action results", and make the
clause expressly subject to the NCA caps.

### 🟠 4.8 Renunciation of surety benefits, in a loan where we are the principal debtor *(cl 8.4)*
We renounce **excussion, division and cession of actions**. These are *surety* defences. Tyron is the
principal debtor, not a surety — so either this is copy‑paste from a suretyship precedent, or it is
laying groundwork to treat him as surety for someone else's obligation later. **Fix:** delete. If
there is genuinely a suretyship intended, it must be a separate, disclosed document.

### 🟠 4.9 They can sell our debt to anyone; we can assign nothing *(Loan cl 21.5, Pledge cl 12.5)*
Loan cl 21.5 restricts **our** cession. Nothing restricts **theirs** — and Pledge cl 12.5 expressly
provides that if Plane Tree cedes the loan, the entire security package follows **"without need for
notice to or prior consent of the Borrower"**. We could find this facility, and every pledge over
Tyron's assets, in the hands of a debt‑collection house or a competitor without ever being told.
**Fix:** at minimum written notice; ideally consent not unreasonably withheld, and an outright bar on
cession to a party that is not a registered credit provider or that competes with our copper
business.

### 🟡 4.10 s129 default notices by registered post *(cl 13.3)*
We consent to the NCA s129 default notice being delivered **by registered post**. Registered post to
an address nobody attends is still deemed delivery — and it starts the 10‑business‑day clock before
they may litigate. **Fix:** require notice by email **and** registered post, with the period running
from the later of the two.

### 🟡 4.11 Shortfall risk with no control over the sale *(cl 1.15, and Pledge cl 5.2.7)*
If the security sells for less than the debt we remain liable for the balance — standard — but Plane
Tree may sell "by public auction, private treaty or **any other commercially reasonable method
determined by the Lender**", with no obligation to obtain a valuation or set a reserve. We carry all
the shortfall risk and have no say in the process. **Fix:** independent valuation before any sale, a
reserve at not less than (say) 70% of that valuation, and 10 business days' notice of any intended
sale so we can find a buyer ourselves.

### 🟡 4.12 "Underlying Transaction" is undefined in substance
Defined only as *"the investments and underlying projects relating to copper for which the Borrower
shall utilise the Maximum Capital Amount"*. Yet cl 6.3.4 requires proceeds be applied **solely** to it,
and cl 14.1.3 makes any breach a default. More seriously, the **Pledge** uses the same phrase to
sweep in every book debt "in connection with any Underlying Transaction" — so an undefined term is
setting the boundary of the security. **Fix:** schedule the specific transaction(s) and define the
term by reference to that schedule.

### 🟡 4.13 Missing NCA prerequisites — their problem, our leverage
Their **comment 7** concedes the pre‑agreement statement and quotation required by NCA s92–93 may not
have been given, and notes: *"A credit agreement cannot be entered into without it."* Their
**comment 31** concedes the s81 affordability assessment has not been done and warns *"a failure to do
so may render the credit agreement void and/or reckless credit."*

These are defects on **their** side. Two consequences for us: (i) we should not sign until the Cost of
Credit table is complete and a proper pre‑agreement quotation has been issued — an agreement with
`R[●]` in it is not a disclosure; and (ii) we should not sign the cl 12.1.3 / 12.1.11 warranties that
are designed to neutralise these very defects.

## 5. Pledge and Cession of Claims — provisions that harm us

### 🔴 5.1 The out‑and‑out cession of the Sale of Shares proceeds *(cl 4.2, 4.4, Annexure A)*
This is the most damaging clause in the package.

Everything else in the document is ceded **`in securitatem debiti`** — as security, ownership stays
with Tyron. But cl 4.2 alone cedes the De Jongh / Nel share‑sale proceeds **"out and out on an
outright basis"** — an absolute transfer of the claim, effective from **signature date**, not on
default.

What that means in practice:

- Those proceeds stop being Tyron's asset the moment he signs — before a cent is drawn down.
- **There is no re‑cession clause.** cl 4.9 only says the agreement stays in force until Plane Tree
  "confirms in writing" the obligations are discharged. For a security cession that is enough. For an
  **out‑and‑out** cession the claim does not simply spring back — it has to be ceded back, and nothing
  in this document obliges them to do that.
- **There is no surplus‑accounting clause for this cession.** cl 5.3 obliges Plane Tree to credit net
  proceeds to us — but cl 5.3 is in the *enforcement* clause and speaks to realising Secured Property
  after an Event of Default. The cl 4.2 cession operates from signature date and sits outside it.
  **If the share sale is worth more than the R2.4m facility, the drafting does not require the excess
  to come back to us.**
- cl 4.4 then requires that if the De Jonghs pay Tyron directly, he must hand the money to Plane Tree
  **within 48 hours** — regardless of whether anything is due.

**Fix — non‑negotiable, in order of preference:**
1. Change cl 4.2 to a cession **`in securitatem debiti`**, on the same footing as cl 4.1; **or**
2. If they insist on out‑and‑out, then all three of: (i) an express **automatic re‑cession** to the
   Borrower on discharge of the Secured Obligations, at Plane Tree's cost and within 10 business days;
   (ii) an express obligation to **account for and pay over any surplus** above the Aggregate Debt
   within 5 business days of receipt; and (iii) an express statement that the cession secures **only**
   the Secured Obligations and no more.

### 🔴 5.2 No "ordinary course" carve‑out — we cannot run the business *(cl 4.1.1–4.1.2, 6.1)*
The Ceded Claims include **all present and future book debts** *and* **"all of the Borrower's claims
against the Borrower's bankers for the net proceeds standing to the credit of the Borrower's
Account"** — i.e. his bank balance.

cl 6.1 then prohibits, without prior written consent:
- 6.1.2 — selling, transferring, **releasing, removing, redirecting** or disposing of any Secured Property;
- 6.1.3 — taking any action, **or omitting to take any action**, that "will, **might** or tends to in any way adversely affect" Plane Tree's interest;
- 6.1.6 — "**divert, redirect, withdraw, transfer, set off, compromise or otherwise deal with** any payment, proceeds, receivable or other amount payable" other than in accordance with "the payment flow approved by the Lender".

**There is no ordinary‑course‑of‑business permission anywhere in the document, and no "payment flow"
has been agreed or attached.** Read literally, from signature date Tyron may not collect a debtor or
draw on his own bank account. In practice a court would strain to avoid that reading, but we should
never rely on a court straining.

**Fix — non‑negotiable:** insert an express clause permitting the Borrower to collect the Ceded Claims
and to operate the Borrower's Account in the ordinary course of business, and to retain and apply the
proceeds, **until an Event of Default has occurred and is continuing** and Plane Tree has given written
notice invoking the cession. This is standard in every properly drafted security cession and its
absence here is conspicuous.

### 🔴 5.3 Enforcement "without notice and without any court" — and the NCA is never mentioned *(cl 5.1)*
cl 5.1: on an Event of Default the security becomes *"immediately enforceable by the Lender in its
discretion **without the need for prior notice to the Borrower or any prior authorisation from any
court or arbitrator**."*

This flatly contradicts:
- **Loan cl 19.2–19.3**, which requires a s129 notice, 20 business days of default and 10 business days from the notice; and
- the **NCA itself** — s129/s130, and s131 for attachment of goods.

Critically: **the word "NCA" and the phrase "National Credit Act" do not appear once in the entire
Pledge and Cession.** The Loan Agreement protects itself with cl 1's statement that the NCA
disclosures prevail over conflicting provisions — but **that prevalence clause lives in the Loan
Agreement and does not reach the Pledge**, which is a separate contract. So on its own four corners
the security document purports to authorise self‑help enforcement against a natural‑person consumer.

**Fix — non‑negotiable, and cheap for them to give:** insert into the Pledge:

> *"This Agreement is subject to the National Credit Act 34 of 2005. To the extent of any conflict
> between this Agreement and the National Credit Act, or clauses 1 or 19 of the Loan Facility
> Agreement, the National Credit Act and those clauses shall prevail."*

That single sentence neutralises a large part of the enforcement risk in this document.

### 🔴 5.4 Perfection on demand — they can take the operating equipment at any time *(cl 4.13, 4.14, 6.2)*
cl 4.13 obliges us, **immediately upon request** and with **no reference to default**, to procure
physical or constructive delivery, control, attornment, endorsement of documents and release
instructions over the Pledged Property. cl 4.14 requires original NATIS documents to be handed over on
request.

The Pledged Property is not idle collateral — it is **the Amarok, the 1TPH induction furnace, the CAT
416F backhoe loader, the rock hammer, and "its inventory from time to time"**. That is the operating
plant. A perfection demand made before any default would stop the business, which would then cause a
default.

**Fix:** make every perfection obligation exercisable **only after an Event of Default that is
continuing**. Before that, we deliver copies of documents, not possession.

### 🟠 5.5 The pledge over "inventory from time to time" *(cl 1.1.10.5)*
An all‑present‑and‑future stock pledge without delivery. Under South African law a pledge of movables
generally requires delivery to be perfected — a promise to pledge future stock does not by itself
create real security. This cuts both ways: it is weak security for them (which is why cl 4.13 is so
aggressive), and it means **they will keep pressing for delivery**. Expect this to be where the
pressure comes from. Consider offering a **special notarial bond** over the four identified assets
instead — it gives them registrable security without stripping us of possession, and it is the
conventional answer to exactly this problem.

### 🟠 5.6 They may take our assets into their own name, valued by their own auditor *(cl 5.2.9)*
Plane Tree may elect to transfer the Secured Property into its own name **as beneficial owner** at
"fair market value". A clause letting a creditor simply keep the security on default (*pactum
commissorium*) is void in South African law; this clause tries to escape that by requiring a fair
value and payment of the surplus. But:
- the valuer, failing agreement in **five business days**, is **"the Lender's auditor"**;
- **we pay that auditor's fees**; and
- the same auditor is the cl 18 arbiter of what we owe (Loan agreement).

So the party enforcing chooses the valuer, we fund the valuer, and the valuer's number is final.
**Fix:** independent valuer jointly appointed, or nominated by the relevant professional body if the
parties cannot agree within 10 business days; costs shared equally; a right for us to obtain and
submit a counter‑valuation.

### 🟠 5.7 Irrevocable power of attorney *in rem suam* to any director of the Lender *(cl 6.3)*
> *"The Borrower hereby irrevocably appoints **any director of the Lender** as its agent with power of
> attorney *in rem suam* to be its lawful attorney and agent, to sign **all such documents and do all
> such things** as may be necessary to give effect to this Agreement."*

Unlimited in subject matter, unlimited in time, irrevocable, exercisable before any default, and held
by *any* director. It would let a Plane Tree director sign documents in Tyron's own name.
**Fix:** limit to (i) exercisable only after an Event of Default that is continuing; (ii) confined to
specified perfection and transfer documents that we have already refused or failed to sign within 5
business days of written request; (iii) written notice to us of each exercise.

### 🟠 5.8 Warranties that are impossible to keep, backed by an uncapped indemnity *(cl 7.1)*

| Clause | Warranty | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1.6 | The Ceded Claims are "valid, enforceable, and **free from dispute**, and there are **no existing or threatened claims, set‑offs, counterclaims, defences**" | The Ceded Claims include **all future** book debts. Nobody can warrant that no future customer will ever dispute an invoice. Every disputed invoice is a breach → Event of Default → acceleration. **Fix:** "so far as the Borrower is aware, as at Signature Date", limited to material claims. |
| 7.1.1 / 7.1.2 | Sole and lawful owner; nothing previously encumbered | **Must be verified before signature — see §6.** If the Amarok or the CAT is under an instalment‑sale/bank finance agreement, this warranty is false on day one and exposes Tyron to a misrepresentation claim, not just a breach. |
| 7.1.8 | All Pledged Property "complies and will comply with all applicable laws, licensing requirements, product specifications, health and safety requirements, storage and transport requirements" | An absolute, forward‑looking regulatory warranty over an 800 kW induction furnace and heavy plant. **Fix:** "in all material respects, so far as the Borrower is aware". |
| 7.1.11 | Indemnity for any loss from breach of any of the above | **Uncapped**, and sits on top of impossible warranties — potential liability well beyond R2.4m. **Fix:** cap at the Aggregate Debt; exclude consequential loss; carve out Plane Tree's own negligence. |

Note also the internal contradiction: cl 4.7 contemplates that some Secured Property **may already be
encumbered** (and cedes the reversionary interest), while cl 7.1.2 warrants that **none of it is**.
They can rely on whichever suits them. **Fix:** make 7.1.2 expressly subject to a disclosure schedule.

### 🟠 5.9 We must deliver a third party's signature within 7 days *(cl 4.3 and Annexure A)*
cl 4.3 obliges us to **procure** that Mila De Jongh, Megan De Jongh, Liam Daniel De Jongh and Andries
Jacobus Nel sign the Annexure A acknowledgement **within 7 days of signature**. They are not parties
to this agreement and are perfectly entitled to refuse. If they refuse, **we breach the Pledge, which
is an Event of Default under the Loan, which accelerates R2.4m.**

We are being made to guarantee the conduct of people we do not control — and note Annexure A also
records that *"the Borrower shall retain any obligations to the Purchasers"*: we keep the burden of
that share sale and lose the benefit.

**Fix:** reduce to "use **reasonable endeavours** to procure", extend to 20 business days, and state
expressly that **failure of the Purchasers to sign is not an Event of Default** (their refusal may, at
most, entitle Plane Tree to require substitute security).

### 🟠 5.10 No release mechanism on repayment
There is **no clause** obliging Plane Tree, once the debt is paid, to release the security, cancel the
cession, re‑cede the ceded claims and return NATIS documents and documents of title. cl 4.9 makes the
agreement run *"until the Lender confirms in writing"* — which puts the trigger entirely in their
hands, with no deadline and no obligation to act. Long term, this is how a settled facility leaves a
lingering encumbrance across an entire estate.

**Fix — non‑negotiable:**
> *"Within 10 business days of the Secured Obligations being discharged in full, the Lender shall, at
> its own cost, deliver to the Borrower a written release and cancellation of this Agreement, re‑cede
> to the Borrower all Ceded Claims, release all Pledged Property, and return all documents of title,
> NATIS documents and other documents delivered to it under this Agreement."*

### 🟡 5.11 No representations outside the document — including negligent ones *(cl 12.4)*
Neither party is bound by any representation not recorded in the agreement, *"irrespective of whether
the representation or warranty induced the Parties to conclude the Agreement and irrespective of
whether the representation or warranty was made **negligently** or not."*

This kills any claim based on what Plane Tree told us verbally about the facility — how flexible the
balloon would be, whether refinance would be available, how enforcement would work in practice.
**Fix:** carve out fraudulent **and reckless** misrepresentation expressly. (Fraud cannot be excluded
in any event; say so, so nobody is under any illusion.)

### 🟡 5.12 Continuing security survives Plane Tree's own failures *(cl 4.10)*
The security survives, among other things, *"the Lender failing to procure or perfect any other form
of collateral"*, *"the Lender abandoning, terminating or releasing any other form of collateral"*, and
*"any other action or omission on the part of the Lender… which might prejudice or adversely affect
the rights of the Borrower."* So if Plane Tree releases other security and thereby increases the
burden on Tyron's assets, he has no complaint. Standard in bank documents, but worth pressing for a
carve‑out for Plane Tree's **gross negligence or wilful misconduct**.

## 7. Negotiating position

**Must have — do not sign without these (seven items):**
1. Payment dates keyed to the **Drawdown Date**, not fixed calendar dates *(Loan cl 8.1)*.
2. **Materiality qualifier + 10 business day cure period** on all non‑payment Events of Default *(Loan cl 14.1)*.
3. Pledge cl 4.2 changed to **`in securitatem debiti`** — or express **re‑cession + surplus‑accounting + cap at the Secured Obligations**.
4. An express **ordinary‑course carve‑out** permitting collection of book debts and operation of the bank account until an Event of Default is continuing *(Pledge cl 6.1)*.
5. An express clause making the Pledge **subject to the NCA**, with the NCA and Loan cl 1 and 19 prevailing *(Pledge cl 5.1)*.
6. A **release and re‑cession clause** on discharge, within 10 business days, at their cost *(Pledge — new clause)*.
7. Interest **not compounded daily**, and the **effective annual rate stated on the face** of the agreement — with the Cost of Credit table completed *(Loan cl 1, cl 9)*.

**Strongly press:**
8. Perfection obligations (delivery, NATIS, attornment) only **after** a continuing Event of Default *(Pledge cl 4.13–4.14)*.
9. Power of attorney narrowed and made post‑default only *(Pledge cl 6.3)*.
10. Independent expert for balance disputes and for fair‑market valuation; costs shared *(Loan cl 18; Pledge cl 5.2.9)*.
11. Warranties qualified by knowledge, materiality and disclosure; **indemnity capped at the Aggregate Debt** *(Pledge cl 7.1)*.
12. Callback verification and a negligence carve‑out on the electronic‑instructions indemnity *(Loan cl 15)*.
13. Negative pledge carved out for asset finance, existing security and a de‑minimis basket *(Loan cl 12.1.7)*.
14. Annexure A obligation reduced to **reasonable endeavours**, and non‑signature expressly not a default *(Pledge cl 4.3)*.
15. Restriction on **Plane Tree** ceding the facility and security onward *(Loan cl 21.5; Pledge cl 12.5)*.
16. Longstop date on suspensive conditions; **delete "Event of Default" from Loan cl 5.3**.

**Trade away if needed:** the plain‑language warranty (12.1.4), the contra proferentem exclusion, the
counterparts/witness provisions, and most of the interpretation clause. These cost us little and give
them the sense of a negotiation.

**A structural question worth putting on the table.** Almost every problem above flows from one
decision: **this is a personal loan to a natural person for what is plainly a business transaction.**
If the copper venture sits in a company, borrowing through that company (with a *capped, defined*
suretyship from Tyron if they insist) would ring‑fence the exposure, remove the personal negative
pledge over all his assets, and take the whole reckless‑credit/affordability problem off the table for
both sides. It also disposes of the s100 legal‑fees problem cleanly. That said — note the trade‑off:
the NCA protections that currently apply because the borrower is a natural person (s129 notice, debt
review, in duplum, prescribed rate caps) would fall away for a company above the NCA threshold. **This
is a judgement call for Dreyer and our attorney, not one to decide in correspondence with Plane Tree.**
