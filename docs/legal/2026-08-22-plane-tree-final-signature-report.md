## 2. What actually changed since 18 August

Only four substantive changes. Everything else in the previous memo stands unchanged.

**Improvements — genuine, and worth acknowledging when you reply:**

1. **Cure periods now exist.** Loan cl 14.1.2 gives 5 business days' written notice to remedy a missed payment; cl 14.1.3 gives 15 business days for any other breach. Previously any breach was an immediate Event of Default with no opportunity to fix it. This was finding #5 in the last memo and it has been properly addressed.
2. **The power of attorney is conditioned.** Pledge cl 6.5 now only lets a Plane Tree director sign in your name (a) during a subsisting, unremedied Event of Default, and (b) after 5 business days' written notice to you to sign yourself. Previously it was a bare, unconditional power of attorney *in rem suam*. Also a real improvement.
3. **Minor softening.** Loan cl 14.1.8 now requires a *material* misstatement that *prejudices the Lender*; cl 14.1.1 no longer treats a waived suspensive condition as a default; cl 6.3.4 broadens permitted use of funds to "any lawful purposes".

**The new exposure:**

4. **A third agreement.** An Acknowledgement of Debt for R74,750 (R65,000 + VAT) has been introduced, with **SB Innovative Construction (Pty) Ltd** as Principal Debtor. Section 6 below deals with it.

**What did *not* change.** The Pledge is otherwise word-for-word the draft. The out-and-out cession of the Sale of Shares proceeds, enforcement without notice or court authorisation, the absence of any ordinary-course carve-out, the lender's free right to cede without your consent, the inverted cooling-off clause, the lender's own accountant as final arbiter of calculation disputes, and the "borrower pays the drafting costs" clause are all still there exactly as they were.

## 3. Blocking item 1 — the disclosed interest does not match the agreement

The Cost of Credit table (previously blank) now reads:

| Line | Disclosed |
|---|---|
| Principal debt | R2 400 000.00 |
| Interest rate | 21% p.a. nominal, compounded daily — fixed |
| Service fee | **NIL** |
| Initiation fee | **NIL** |
| Total interest, fees and charges | **R688 013.32** (fees & charges R99 613.33; interest R588 399.99) |
| Total cost of credit | **R3 088 013.32** |

Two problems.

### 3.1 The interest figure ignores your instalments

Clause 8.1 requires twelve instalments of R100,000 (31 August 2026 through 31 July 2027), with the balance due 31 August 2027. Clause 9.2 says interest is "calculated daily on the **outstanding balance**". Clause 8.5 allocates every payment to interest first, then fees, then capital.

Run those clauses and the numbers come out like this:

| Drawdown assumption | Interest over the term | Balloon due 31 Aug 2027 | Total repaid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 Aug 2026 | R428 701 | R1 628 701 | R2 828 701 |
| 5 Sep 2026 | R405 187 | R1 605 187 | R2 805 187 |
| 19 Sep 2026 (latest under cl 5 + 6.2) | R382 497 | R1 582 497 | R2 782 497 |

*(Model output on the agreement's own terms — daily compounding at 0.21/365, instalments applied on their due dates. To be confirmed against the lender's own calculation.)*

The disclosed R588,399.99 sits far above all of these. It corresponds to daily compounding on **the full, undiminished R2,400,000 for roughly 381 days** — i.e. as if the twelve R100,000 instalments were never paid at all. (For reference: R2.4m compounded daily for 381 days is R588,021; simple interest at 21% for fourteen months is R588,000.)

**The gap is roughly R183,000 in interest, and roughly R283,000 in total cost of credit.** The disclosed total implies a balloon of **R1,888,013** on 31 August 2027 rather than the ~R1.6m the repayment clauses produce.

Either the table is a worst-case, non-amortising disclosure (in which case it should say so), or the lender intends to charge interest on the original capital regardless of what you repay — which flatly contradicts clause 9.2. **Get this in writing before signature.** Ask for the amortisation schedule behind the R588,399.99, showing the assumed drawdown date and how each R100,000 instalment is applied.

### 3.2 R99,613.33 of "fees and charges" is unexplained and contradicts the same table

The table records SERVICE FEE **NIL** and INITIATION FEE **NIL**, then discloses R99,613.33 of "Fees & Charges". Nothing in the agreement identifies what these are. Note also that R99,613.33 is neither the R74,750 in the AOD nor any multiple of it.

This matters beyond the money. Under the NCA a credit provider may only charge the amounts the Act permits, and the initiation and service fee caps are a fraction of this figure. An unexplained six-figure charge line, sitting directly beneath two NIL entries in the statutory disclosure table, is the kind of defect that undermines the disclosure as a whole.

**Ask for an itemised written breakdown of the R99,613.33 before signature**, and confirmation that it is not the same legal cost being recovered a second time through the AOD.

## 4. Blocking item 2 — the first instalment falls due before you are funded

This was finding #4 last time. It has now become near-certain rather than theoretical, purely because of the calendar.

- Clause 8.1.1: R100,000 due **on or before 31 August 2026**.
- Clause 5.1: suspensive conditions must be met within **14 calendar days after signature** — so up to ~5 September if you sign on 22 August.
- Clause 6.2: the R2.4m is paid **within 14 days of the Effective Date** — so up to ~19 September.
- Clause 14.1.2: failure to pay on the due date, uncured after 5 business days' notice, is an Event of Default that accelerates the entire debt and lets the lender execute against every pledged asset.

On the documents as drafted you can be in default on a R2.4m facility **before a cent has been advanced to you**. The new 5-business-day cure period helps but does not solve it — you would be curing a payment obligation on money you do not have.

**Fix:** amend clause 8.1.1 so the first instalment is due on the last day of the first full calendar month *following the date of actual drawdown*, and make every subsequent date run off that. This is a mechanical correction and there is no honest reason for the lender to resist it.

While that clause is open, also press for a **long-stop on funding**: clause 6.2 obliges the lender to pay "within 14 days of the Effective Date" but gives you no remedy at all if they do not. Ask for a right to walk away, at no cost and with the security released, if the money has not landed by a fixed date.

## 5. Blocking item 3 — the Sale of Shares cession still has no way home

Unchanged from the last memo, and still the largest structural risk in the package.

Every other asset is ceded **in securitatem debiti** — as security, with your underlying ownership intact. The Sale of Shares proceeds are the single exception: Pledge clause 4.2 cedes them **"out and out on an outright basis"**. Annexure A now goes further and instructs Mila De Jongh, Megan De Jongh, Liam Daniel De Jongh and Andries Jacobus Nel to pay Plane Tree directly, while Annexure A clause 7 keeps *your* obligations to them fully in place.

There is still **no clause requiring the claim to be ceded back to you when the loan is settled**, and no clause obliging the lender to account to you for anything the purchasers pay over and above what you owe. Clause 5.3 credits net collections *against the debt*; clause 5.2.9's surplus mechanism applies only where the lender elects to take transfer as beneficial owner.

Two consequences worth being clear-eyed about:

- **If you repay in full**, nothing in the documents automatically returns the claim to you or redirects the purchasers back to your account. You would be dependent on the lender's cooperation.
- **If Plane Tree is wound up**, an out-and-out cession means the claim sits in *their* insolvent estate, not yours. You would rank as a concurrent creditor for the excess.

**Fix:** a short side letter — or two inserted clauses — providing that (a) on full settlement of the Aggregate Debt the claim re-cedes to you automatically and the lender notifies the purchasers in writing within 5 business days, and (b) the lender accounts to you for any amount received in excess of the Aggregate Debt within 5 business days of receipt. If the lender genuinely takes this cession only as security, neither clause costs them anything.

## 6. Blocking item 4 — the AOD is the wrong debtor, and it is the item to fight

This is new, and it is the one that most directly cuts against the instruction not to create exposure beyond this transaction.

**What it does.** SB Innovative Construction (Pty) Ltd acknowledges owing Plane Tree **R74,750** (R65,000 + VAT), payable by **15 September 2026**, with 21% p.a. compounded and capitalised **daily** on any unpaid balance thereafter.

**Why this is a problem:**

1. **Wrong debtor.** SB Innovative is not a party to the loan, receives none of the R2.4m, and gets no benefit from it. The debt being acknowledged is the cost of drafting *Tyron's personal* facility.
2. **It looks like a workaround.** In the margin comments on the 13 August draft, the lender's own drafter recorded that these drafting costs cannot lawfully be charged to the consumer. Loan clause 16.1 nevertheless still says the Borrower pays all drafting costs. Moving the same cost into a separate acknowledgement against a *company* — where the NCA's consumer protections may not reach — is the obvious explanation for this document's existence, and it should be named as such in your reply.
3. **The renunciations are aggressive for a debtor in this position.** Clause 6.1 has SB Innovative renounce *non causa debiti* (it cannot later argue there is no underlying cause for the claim) and *non numeratae pecuniae* (it cannot argue it received no value) — precisely the two defences a company in SB Innovative's position would otherwise have. It also renounces *errore calculi* and revision of accounts. Note the clause repeatedly refers to a "surety" although there is no surety anywhere in the document — sloppy drafting that should itself be corrected.
4. **Consent to judgment without notice.** Clause 9.1 has SB Innovative consent to the agreement being made an order of court by **ex parte application, without notice**, at SB Innovative's expense. Combined with the clause 7 certificate provision, that is a fast route to an enforceable judgment against a group company over a R74,750 fee dispute.
5. **A pure-discretion default trigger.** Clause 4.1.8 makes it a breach if SB Innovative "causes or allows a situation to arise wherein the Creditor **in its sole discretion** considers its position as creditor in any way prejudiced or threatened either now or **at any time in the future**." No cure period attaches to it. That is not a default clause; it is an at-will acceleration right.

**Recommended position, in order of preference:**

- **First choice:** the legal fee is Plane Tree's own cost of putting up the facility and should be absorbed, or netted off the advance as a properly disclosed charge inside the loan agreement where the NCA governs it. Decline the separate AOD.
- **Second choice:** if a fee is genuinely agreed, pay it as a once-off invoice on drawdown. There is no reason for a twelve-page acknowledgement of debt with consent to judgment over R74,750.
- **Last resort, if it must be signed:** delete clause 9 (ex parte order of court) entirely, delete clause 4.1.8, delete the *non causa debiti* and *non numeratae pecuniae* renunciations, correct the "surety" references, and make the debtor Tyron personally rather than SB Innovative — so the exposure stays inside the transaction it belongs to.

**Also confirm** whether the R74,750 is inside or outside the R99,613.33 disclosed in the loan's Cost of Credit table. If it is inside, you are being asked to pay it twice.

## 7. Items to accept with eyes open

These are unattractive but are ordinary in secured lending, and are unlikely to be conceded. Listed so the decision is deliberate rather than accidental.

| Clause | Effect |
|---|---|
| Pledge 5.1 | Security enforceable on default "without the need for prior notice… or any prior authorisation from any court or arbitrator". The Pledge does not mention the NCA anywhere — the word does not appear in the document. The loan's s129 machinery does not on its face reach it. Legal view needed on whether s91 pulls the Pledge in as a supplementary agreement. |
| Pledge 6.1 | No ordinary-course-of-business carve-out. Your book debts *and* the credit balance in your FNB account are ceded, and clause 6.1.6 bars you from dealing with any payment except through a "payment flow approved by the Lender" — a flow that does not yet exist. Ask for it in writing before signature, or for an express ordinary-course carve-out. |
| Pledge 12.6 | Plane Tree may cede its rights to any third party without notice to you or your consent; the security follows automatically. You may not cede anything without their consent. |
| Loan 18 | Calculation disputes go to **the lender's own accountant**, whose determination is "final and binding". Given section 3 above, this is the clause you would need to use — and it sends the dispute to the other side's advisers. Press for an independent auditor agreed between the parties. |
| Loan 12.1.7 | Negative pledge over **all** your assets for the whole term. Check this against any other facility or supplier arrangement you have. |
| Loan 11 | Broad audit and inspection rights over your books, at your cost, with failure to provide information a *material breach*. |
| Pledge 7.1.6 + 7.1.11 | You warrant the ceded claims are "free from dispute" with no set-offs or counterclaims, and indemnify the lender for any breach — an absolute warranty about how third parties will behave. |
| Loan 1.16 | The cooling-off clause is **still drafted backwards**: it keys off "the registered business premises of **the Borrower**" where s121 keys off the *credit provider's* premises. As drafted it is unlikely to give you a usable 5-business-day right, and in any event it would expire before funding. Worth a one-word correction on principle. |
| Loan 16.1 / Pledge 9.1 | You pay all drafting, implementation and cancellation costs. See section 6. |
